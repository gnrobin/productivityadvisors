The Diagnostic

Growing companies often know they need ops help but can't say what kind. Over four weeks we look at the people, the process and the systems behind their revenue, then hand the team a plan for what to fix, what to buy and who to hire.

Why Diagnose First

When revenue operations start to creak, the usual reflex is to hire someone or buy a tool. Often neither fixes it, because the mess shows up in Salesforce while the cause sits in ownership, hand-offs or definitions that nobody agreed on.

The Diagnostic is a fixed-scope way to find out what is actually broken before anyone spends real money. It is light on the team, mostly interviews and system access, and it ends with a plan the leadership team can act on.

Three Lenses

What we look at

Goal: look past the software to the people and process behind it.

People

Who owns what, and is the team bought in?

ProducesA stakeholder findings summary with themes, quotes and where leaders disagree, plus a pain-point heat map by role and stage.

Process

How do deals, hand-offs and data really move?

ProducesAs-is and future-state swim lane maps from lead to cash, with every break and hand-off gap marked.

Systems

Do the tools support the work or get in its way?

ProducesA Salesforce audit of configuration, data quality, automation, integrations and adoption.

How each area is scored

Twelve areas across people, process, systems and data are each rated 1 to 4. Every score is backed by evidence: a quote, a query result or a step on the process map.

ScoreWhat it looks like
1 Ad hocDepends on individuals; work happens outside the system
2 InconsistentRules exist on paper but are often bypassed
3 DefinedAgreed, documented and mostly followed
4 ScaledMeasured, owned and improved on a schedule

How the Four Weeks Run

Weeks 1–4

Goal: listen, map, score and prioritize, in that order.

WeekThe workWhat the team gives
1. ListenKickoff, system access and the first stakeholder interviewsA sponsor, an interview list and read access to Salesforce
2. MapFinish interviews and map the as-is processThirty minutes with process owners to check the maps
3. ScoreSalesforce audit, scorecard and the future-state designA check-in with the sponsor on early findings
4. PrioritizeRoadmap and the leadership readoutNinety minutes with the leadership team
Out of scope during the four weeks: building or fixing anything. The point is a clear picture first. Delivery can follow if the company wants it.

Why It's an Easy Referral

For VC and PE operating teams

  • Clarity before capital, so a portfolio company spends on the right hire or tool once
  • One repeatable method across the portfolio, so findings are comparable
  • A light lift for the company: mostly interviews and system access

For recruiters

  • A useful next step for companies that aren't ready to hire yet
  • A sharper job spec when they are, because the role is named by the work
  • A plan the new hire can pick up on day one

The Takeaway

Most revenue system problems are people and process problems that happen to show up in software. Four weeks of listening, mapping and scoring turns "we need ops help" into a ranked plan. Find out what's broken, then decide whether to fix, buy or hire.
