Parachuting In

A first-90-days playbook for GTM operators taking on a new role, client or project. The thesis: data, then automation, then AI. In that order, with a gate between each.

Why the First 90 Days Matter

Whether it is a full-time seat, a fractional engagement or a single project, your first 90 days decide how people see you for the next two years. Everyone will want to talk about AI. The operators who do well start somewhere less exciting: they find out which numbers can be trusted, fix the hand-offs, and only then build AI on a foundation that can hold it.

This is the sequence we use. Each 30-day phase has a goal, the work to do, and what you should have in hand when it ends.

The Ladder: Four Steps, Each With a Gate

Don't move up a step until you've passed the gate below it.

1

Diagnose

Days 1–30

Goal: listen and measure before you change anything.

Who to meet

  • Leaders in Sales, Marketing, CS, Delivery, Finance and Legal. Ask each for their top three system pains and the report they do not trust.
  • The admins and whoever built the CRM. They know why things are the way they are.
  • Three to five reps and managers. Watch them work a deal; do not just ask.

Which numbers to trust

  • Put pipeline, bookings, ARR and forecast next to Finance's numbers. Where they differ, find the definition that differs.
  • Score data quality on the fields the forecast depends on: amount, close date, stage, owner.
  • Treat any number nobody can trace back to records as a hypothesis.

Where lead-to-cash breaks

  • Walk one real deal from first touch to cash collected. List every system, hand-off and spreadsheet.
  • Mark where data is retyped, where approvals stall and where Finance redoes the work.
  • Those marks become your roadmap.
By day 30: one intake channel with a weekly triage, plus a written readout: systems map, data quality scorecard, top ten issues ranked, and what you will not touch yet.
2

Stabilize

Days 30–60

Goal: fix the foundation and earn trust.

Fix the data and the definitions

  1. Publish a one-page glossary (stage, bookings, recurring vs. one-time revenue, churn) signed off by Finance and Sales leadership.
  2. Enforce it in the system with validation rules, picklists and required fields, so the definitions hold after you leave the room.
  3. Clean the forecast fields first. Retire unused fields and automations in waves, with usage counts behind each removal.
  4. Fix the worst hand-off on your lead-to-cash map.
  5. Draft the systems roadmap and a vendor renewal calendar, with a build vs. buy view for each tool.

Pick two or three quick wins

Visible, low risk, shipped in under two weeks. Good ones look like this:

Quick winWho notices
Forecast view that matches FinanceCRO and CFO; ends the "whose number" debate
Quote, contract and billing checkFinance, before the first invoice; fewer credits and rebills
Next steps filled from call notesReps and managers; next steps and contacts kept current, time back for selling
Request intake with status updatesEvery stakeholder; they feel heard
By day 60: agreed definitions, a cleaner baseline with a quality score, two or three shipped wins, and a draft roadmap.
3

Scale

Days 60–90

Goal: automate first, then pilot AI and prove it.

How to choose and run pilots

  1. Automate the rule-based work first. Use a flow when the rule is clear and an LLM when the input is messy. Most first-year wins are the first kind.
  2. Score every AI idea on two things: build effort and adoption effort. Many GTM AI projects are easy to build and hard to adopt. Start with ideas low on both.
  3. Check the AI already in your tools before building anything new.
  4. Set each pilot up to be measured: a baseline taken before launch, a small golden set of real examples, a rubric to grade the output, and a named owner who uses it every week.
  5. Report against the baseline. Scale the winner and stop the loser.

Pilots worth starting with

PilotHow you measure it
CRM hygiene from calls and emailShare of open deals with a current next step and contact roles; admin time per rep
Deal risk and forecast narrativeForecast accuracy against prior quarters; time spent in forecast calls
Contract term extractionFields filled without rework; non-standard clauses caught
Ops request triageTime to first response; duplicate requests
By day 90: an approved 12-month roadmap, a release cadence with notes for stakeholders and Enablement, and pilot results with numbers.

Toolkit

Diagnostic checklist

Data

  • Pipeline, bookings and forecast reconcile to Finance
  • Stage definitions written down and enforced
  • Quality score on forecast-critical fields
  • Account and contact duplicate rate known

Systems

  • Systems map with an owner for every tool
  • Field and automation usage audited
  • Integration users on least privilege
  • Renewal date and cost for every vendor

Process

  • One real deal traced from lead to cash
  • Every manual hand-off and spreadsheet listed
  • One intake channel for requests
  • A release cadence with change notes

AI readiness

  • Rules for customer data in AI tools agreed with Legal and Security
  • Native AI in current tools reviewed
  • Baselines captured before any pilot
  • A business owner for each use case

Red flags and what they usually mean

What you seeWhat it usually means
Three versions of the pipeline numberDefinitions were never agreed, and Sales and Finance do not trust each other's data
"We tried AI and it didn't work"It was built on dirty data or placed outside the daily workflow
Hundreds of custom fields, half emptyNo governance; every request got a new field
The real reports live in spreadsheetsThe CRM does not reflect how deals actually work
One admin who "knows how it all works"A single point of failure and undocumented automation
New tools bought every quarterOverlapping features and no one owning renewals
No six-month definition of successThe role or project is not scoped yet. Get that answered before anything else

Questions to ask

In the interview or kickoff

  • What would make this a success at six months?
  • How has the GTM motion changed in the last year, and which systems broke first?
  • How does a deal get scoped and priced today, and where do Delivery and Finance come in?
  • What has the team tried with AI, and what stuck?
  • Who built the CRM, and what state is it in?

In the first week

  • Which report do you not trust, and why?
  • Where do you retype data from one system into another?
  • What request have you given up asking for?
  • What are the rules for putting customer data, call recordings and contracts into AI tools?
  • Which vendors renew in the next two quarters?

The Takeaway

AI in GTM is a data problem first and an adoption problem second. The build is usually the easy part. Lead with AI and you will ship demos. Lead with the data and the hand-offs and you will ship things people use, and the AI work that follows will have numbers behind it. Earn the right to automate, then earn the right to use AI.
