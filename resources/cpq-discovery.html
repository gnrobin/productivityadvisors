Framing

Why CPQ Discovery Comes First

  • CPQ encodes your commercial model: price, package, discount, terms. When those decisions are unmade, the build makes them by accident.
  • Most CPQ pain isn't the tool. It's undefined deal types, unwritten discount policy, and no agreed definition of ARR.
  • Part 1 is a 12-topic discovery rubric. Part 2 is the build blueprint those answers unlock, across eight functional areas.

Section A · Commercial Model A1

Pricing & Packaging Strategy

Point of view
  • CPQ encodes a pricing strategy; it can't invent one. If packaging changes every quarter, you're building a configurator for a moving target.
  • Decide the value metric (seat, usage, tier, platform fee) before the catalog. The metric drives every rule downstream.
Discovery questions
  1. 1What's your value metric, and would customers agree it tracks the value they get?
  2. 2How many price changes or new SKUs shipped in the last 12 months, and who approved them?
  3. 3Is pricing published list, or negotiated down from a ceiling? Do reps know which?
Learning: Pricing rules are cheap to change. A catalog built around the wrong metric is a re-implementation.
Section A · Commercial Model A2

Product Catalog & Bundles

Point of view
  • The catalog is the contract between Product, Sales and Finance: every SKU needs a charge type, a revenue treatment and an owner.
  • Fewer, cleaner SKUs beat a SKU per deal. Bundles should mirror how customers buy, not how the org chart is drawn.
Discovery questions
  1. 1How many active SKUs do you have, and how many actually sold in the last 12 months?
  2. 2For each SKU: one-time, recurring or usage? Who decides, and where is it written?
  3. 3Which products require, exclude, or re-price other products when sold together?
Learning: If Finance keeps its own SKU map in a spreadsheet, the catalog isn't done. It's duplicated.
Section A · Commercial Model A3

Deal Types & Motions

Point of view
  • New, add-on, upgrade, ramp, trial, co-term, renewal: each deal type is a distinct CPQ path with its own pricing, approvals and downstream records.
  • Name the motions you actually run. Every unnamed one becomes a workaround inside the quote.
Discovery questions
  1. 1List every deal shape sold last quarter: ramps, multi-year, trials, partner, mid-term add-ons. Which ones broke the quote?
  2. 2How do trials and pilots convert: a new quote, or an amendment of the same contract?
  3. 3What share of bookings flows through partners or resellers, and at what margin?
Learning: The 10% of deals that don't fit the standard path eat most of Deal Desk's week. Name them before you build.
Section B · Deal Governance B1

Discounting Policy

Point of view
  • A discount is a pricing decision delegated to a rep. Delegation needs limits by %, deal size, term and product line.
  • Separate system discounts (volume, term, partner) from discretionary ones. You can only govern what you can see.
Discovery questions
  1. 1What's the average discretionary discount by segment, and has it drifted up over four quarters?
  2. 2Do you discount at the line, the quote or the total, and does the order form show it the same way?
  3. 3Which discount reasons are allowed, and are they captured on the quote?
Learning: An unwritten discount policy isn't flexibility. It's a price list nobody wrote down.
Section B · Deal Governance B2

Approval Matrix

Point of view
  • An approval matrix is policy you can query: every threshold maps to an approver, a reason and a turnaround time.
  • Design for approval avoidance first. Most deals should need no approval; approvals exist to flag exceptions.
Discovery questions
  1. 1What share of quotes route for approval today, and what share are ever rejected?
  2. 2How long does the median approval take, and where does it stall?
  3. 3Which non-price terms need sign-off: payment terms, term length, free months, opt-outs?
Learning: If 80% of quotes need approval and 1% get rejected, you've built a delay, not a control.
Section B · Deal Governance B3

Quote Documents & Terms

Point of view
  • The quote document is the customer's contract. Merge fields, clause logic and e-signature status are part of the build, not cosmetics.
  • Standard terms should be defaults. Non-standard terms should be data on the quote so Legal and Finance can report on them.
Discovery questions
  1. 1How many quote templates exist, and how many are still in use?
  2. 2When Legal edits terms on a deal, where does that language live afterward?
  3. 3After signature, what locks: the quote, the opportunity, both or neither?
Learning: Special terms trapped in a PDF are invisible at renewal. Put them in a field.
Section C · Quote-to-Cash Flow C1

Orders & Contracts

Point of view
  • Closed-won isn't the finish line; the order and contract are. They carry effective dates, subscriptions and the data billing depends on.
  • One quote may become several orders, split by entity, billing schedule or start date. Decide the split rules before you build.
Discovery questions
  1. 1What's the system of record for an active subscription: CRM, billing or a spreadsheet?
  2. 2When a deal closes, what happens in the next 24 hours, and who does it by hand?
  3. 3Do you ever split one deal into multiple orders or invoices? Why?
Learning: Every manual step between signature and invoice becomes a revenue-recognition question at audit time.
Section C · Quote-to-Cash Flow C2

Billing, ERP & Provisioning

Point of view
  • CPQ is upstream of revenue. Billing, ERP and provisioning all consume what the quote produces, so define the handoff field by field.
  • Choose the integration pattern (native billing, iPaaS, direct API) by who owns the fix when it breaks.
Discovery questions
  1. 1Which systems consume a closed deal: billing, ERP, provisioning, CS platform?
  2. 2When the invoice doesn't match the order form, who notices first: Finance or the customer?
  3. 3Who owns the integration when it fails at 11pm on the last day of the quarter?
Learning: Integrations rarely fail loudly. They fail by quietly booking the wrong amount, so build reconciliation in from day one.
Section C · Quote-to-Cash Flow C3

Amendments & Renewals

Point of view
  • The subscription lifecycle (upsell, downgrade, co-term, cancel, renew) is where most CPQ projects go wrong. Design it first, not last.
  • Renewals should generate themselves from contract data, with uplift set by policy rather than rep memory.
Discovery questions
  1. 1How do you handle a mid-term add-on today: co-termed, a separate contract, or case by case?
  2. 2What's your renewal uplift policy, and what share of renewals actually carry it?
  3. 3How far ahead of end date is the renewal opportunity created, and who owns it?
Learning: New-business quoting is the demo. Amendments and renewals are the real product.
Section D · Revenue Data & Platform D1

ARR & Revenue Metrics

Point of view
  • Calculate ARR at the quote line and roll it up through subscription, contract and account. Never re-derive it in a spreadsheet.
  • Agree definitions first: Current, Prior and Delta ARR, and how ramps, trials, one-time fees and credits count.
Discovery questions
  1. 1Do Sales, Finance and the board report the same ARR number? What reconciles them?
  2. 2How does a ramp deal count in ARR: year-one value, final-year value or average?
  3. 3Can you see Account ARR on the account record today without running a report?
Learning: If ARR lives in Finance's model and not on the Account, Sales is forecasting a number it can't see.
Section D · Revenue Data & Platform D2

Current State & Pain

Point of view
  • Most CPQ projects are re-implementations. Understand what broke, and why, before you redesign it.
  • Separate process pain (unclear policy) from tool pain (wrong config) from data pain (dirty history). Each needs a different fix.
Discovery questions
  1. 1What are the three quote problems Deal Desk fixes by hand every week?
  2. 2How many custom fields, price rules and code-based triggers live on the quote today, and who built them?
  3. 3If you could fix one thing in quoting tomorrow, what would it be?
Learning: A CPQ rebuild that copies today's workarounds just automates the mess.
Section D · Revenue Data & Platform D3

Platform Fit

Point of view
  • The commercial model picks the platform. Seat-and-term SaaS, usage and credits, and PLG-plus-sales hybrids each point to different tools.
  • Legacy Salesforce CPQ still runs many orgs but is end-of-sale for new customers. Weigh migration timing against how much you're about to change anyway.
Discovery questions
  1. 1Is your CRM Salesforce, HubSpot or both, and is that likely to change within two years?
  2. 2How much revenue is usage- or credit-based today, and how much will be in two years?
  3. 3Is billing in scope for the same platform, or staying in the ERP?
Learning: Pick the platform for the pricing model you'll have in two years, not the one you had at your last renewal.

Build 1 · Quoting Process Flow

Quoting Process Flow

Build requirements
  • Guided opportunity creation that sets stage, type and subtype up front
  • Billing and shipping addresses sourced from the primary contact or account
  • Staggered and ramp deals via quote-line start and end dates
  • Account ARR on the Account; Opportunity ARR on the Opportunity
  • Partner / reseller flag on the quote that drives pricing and paper
  • Revenue metrics (Current, Prior, Delta ARR, TCV) on quote and opportunity
  • Layouts built around approval status and the e-signature flow
Discovery inputs
A3 Deal Types & MotionsD1 ARR & Revenue MetricsB3 Quote Documents & Terms
Platform notes

SBQQ / RCA: Screen Flows and record types set deal type at creation. DealHub / Nue: guided playbooks on the quote. Confirm deal type writes back to the CRM opportunity.

Learning: Ask what kind of deal it is once, at creation. Every downstream rule depends on that answer.
Build 2 · Products & Configuration

Products & Configuration

Build requirements
  • Rationalized catalog: every SKU tagged with charge type, revenue treatment and owner
  • Bundles, options and product rules (requires / excludes / recommends)
  • Trials configured as products, with a defined conversion path
  • Quote line experience tailored by deal type: new, amend, renew
  • Line groups for ramps, phases and multi-entity deals
Discovery inputs
A2 Product Catalog & BundlesA3 Deal Types & MotionsA1 Pricing & Packaging Strategy
Platform notes

SBQQ: bundles, features, product rules. RCA: Product Catalog Management plus configurator rules. DealHub: no-code playbook questions. Nue: bundles with subscription, usage and credit charge models.

Learning: Every SKU without a charge type and revenue treatment becomes a Finance ticket later.
Build 3 · Pricing & Discounting

Pricing & Discounting

Build requirements
  • Pricing aligned to the platform's standard waterfall; no custom math where a standard step exists
  • Line-level discounting standardized: %, amount, target total, unit override
  • Pricing rules and constraints built on platform best practice
  • Partner pricing: revenue share and automatic partner discounts
  • Discount reason required on every discretionary discount
  • Quote pricing summary: Current, Prior, Delta ARR and TCV
  • Trial pricing, rounding regression tests, price-management governance
Discovery inputs
A1 Pricing & Packaging StrategyB1 Discounting PolicyD1 ARR & Revenue Metrics
Platform notes

SBQQ: price waterfall, price rules, discount schedules. RCA: pricing procedures. DealHub / Nue: native price books and tables. Map each waterfall step to a named field on every platform.

Later / dependencies

Multi-currency: settle scope in discovery before configuring price books.

Learning: If a price can't be explained step by step on the waterfall, the rep can't defend it and Finance can't audit it.
Build 3 · Pricing & Discounting

The Pricing Waterfall

How the price is built

Standard item
Extended price = unit × quantity

Subscription item
Extended price = unit × quantity × prorated term

Each step is a named, reportable field. System discounts come from schedules; the rep controls one step only.

Net price flows to pipeline and forecast amount.

Build 4 · Quote Approvals

Quote Approvals

Build requirements
  • Discount approvals by threshold and tier
  • Free-seat and free-month approvals
  • Term, payment and legal approvals (e.g. term over 36 months, non-standard payment terms)
  • Approval rules on amendments, renewals and trials
  • Notifications for request, rejection, recall and approval
  • Pricing engine calculates the metrics that approval rules evaluate
  • Smart approvals (no re-approval when nothing material changed); approver notes and change tracking
Discovery inputs
B1 Discounting PolicyB2 Approval Matrix
Platform notes

SBQQ: Advanced Approvals package with smart approvals. RCA: Flow-based approval orchestration. DealHub / Nue: built-in approval workflows; confirm the audit trail lands in the CRM.

Learning: Approvals should evaluate calculated fields, never rep-typed ones. Otherwise the matrix is a suggestion.

Design principles

Quote Templates & E-signature

Build requirements

  • Conditional T&Cs and special clauses driven by quote data
  • Template refresh: new columns and merge fields
  • Send for e-signature directly from the quote
  • Quote status synced to e-signature envelope status
  • Quote and opportunity locked once the primary quote is out for signature

Build 6 · Order & Contract Management

Order & Contract Management

Build requirements
  • Automated order creation from the closed-won opportunity
  • Order splitting: one quote to multiple orders
  • True effective dates captured on orders
  • Amendments carry only line-item deltas
  • Quote and quote-line data staged onto orders for ERP and billing
  • Contract created from the order, aligned to standard quote-to-cash
  • Renewal uplift and renewal data tracked on the contract
  • Integration staging for iPaaS; framework for provisioning from order data
Discovery inputs
C1 Orders & ContractsC2 Billing, ERP & Provisioning
Platform notes

SBQQ: Order, Contract, Subscriptions. RCA: Dynamic Revenue Orchestrator for fulfillment. DealHub / Nue: subscription records sync to CRM and billing; decide which is the system of record.

Later / dependencies

Later: uplift control at subscription level; a co-terming process for accounts with multiple contracts.

Learning: Stage the ERP payload on the order now, even if the integration comes later. Backfilling history is the expensive part.
Build 7 · Amendments

Amendments

Build requirements
  • Use the platform's standard amendment framework
  • Upgrades, upsells and price-reduction scenarios
  • Accurate pricing when crediting existing lines and adding new ones
  • Amendments never create duplicate contracts
  • Effective quantity and amendment start-date management
  • Clawbacks: cancellation, finance cancel, refunds, re-booking in a new currency, expired-contract fixes
  • Amend-and-extend; amendment data visible for forecasting and reporting
Discovery inputs
C3 Amendments & RenewalsA3 Deal Types & MotionsD1 ARR & Revenue Metrics
Platform notes

SBQQ: amend from contract; watch large-quote limits. RCA: asset-based lifecycle, where assets replace subscriptions. DealHub / Nue: native change orders; test co-term math on real contracts.

Later / dependencies

Later: ERP return-and-rebill process. Non-standard cases may need code.

Learning: Test amendments on your five ugliest real contracts before go-live, not on clean demo data.
Build 8 · Renewals

Renewals

Build requirements
  • Renewals generated with a default uplift %, overridable per subscription
  • Contract and subscription data carried to the renewal opportunity and quote
  • Renewal opportunity and quote auto-created 90 days before contract end
  • Renewals in a different currency than the original contract
  • ARR calculated on renewal price points
  • Renewals use the standard pricing waterfall and discount flow
  • Known renewal pricing defects fixed and regression-tested
Discovery inputs
C3 Amendments & RenewalsD1 ARR & Revenue MetricsB2 Approval Matrix
Platform notes

SBQQ: renewal forecast and renewal quoted flags on the contract. RCA: asset-based renewals. DealHub / Nue: auto-renewal jobs; align uplift and notice windows to contract terms.

Learning: A renewal created 90 days out with the right uplift is a forecast. One created at expiry is a scramble.
Build 6–8 · Lifecycle & ARR

ARR Logic Across the Lifecycle

One calculation at the quote line, rolled up through subscriptions. New, amend and renew quotes all compute Current, Prior and Delta ARR the same way.
