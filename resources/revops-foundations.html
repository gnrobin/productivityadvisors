Framing

Why RevOps Now

  • Between $2M and $20M ARR, what got you here (founder selling, hustle, tribal knowledge) stops scaling. Repeatability becomes the product.
  • RevOps is decisions first, tooling second: who you sell to, how deals move, who covers what, and how you inspect it. Salesforce then encodes those decisions.
  • This deck is a discovery rubric: a short point of view per topic, and the questions I'd work through with your team.

Section A · Strategy & Focus A1

Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)

Point of view
  • Your ICP is an operating decision, not a marketing slide. It should drive routing, territories, comp and forecast segmentation.
  • Define it by who buys fastest, retains best and expands. Not by who happens to have bought.
Discovery questions
  1. 1Can you describe your ICP in fields Salesforce can filter on: industry, employee band, geo, tech stack?
  2. 2Do your best-retaining customers match your stated ICP, or just your biggest logos?
  3. 3Where does ICP fit live today: a score or tier on the Account, or in people's heads?
Learning: If ICP isn't data on the Account, it isn't operational. It's a slide. A simple tier field beats a beautiful persona doc.
Section A · Strategy & Focus A2

Segments & Geographies

Point of view
  • Segmentation is how you decide where reps spend their time. At $2–20M, narrow beats broad.
  • New geographies usually arrive by accident (EMEA inbound) before they arrive by strategy, dragging comp, coverage and data decisions behind them.
Discovery questions
  1. 1Which segment has your best win rate and shortest cycle, and does your headcount allocation reflect it?
  2. 2What's pulling you into new geographies: deliberate strategy, or opportunistic inbound?
  3. 3If you had to double down on one segment for the next 12 months, which one, and why?
Learning: Every segment you name should have a distinct motion. If it only has a distinct label, it's not a segment. It's a report filter.
Section A · Strategy & Focus A3

New, Expand & Renew Motions

Point of view
  • New, expand and renew are three different sales processes, with different stages, owners and forecast categories. Not one blended pipeline.
  • Expansion and renewal are the cheapest growth you have. Most teams just haven't instrumented them yet.
Discovery questions
  1. 1Are new business, expansion and renewal separate Opportunity record types in Salesforce, or one pipeline pretending?
  2. 2Who owns the renewal, and when is the opportunity created: at close, 120 days out, or never?
  3. 3Can you report NRR and expansion pipeline today without opening a spreadsheet?
Learning: Record types cost an afternoon to build. Rebuilding NRR history out of blended closed deals costs a quarter.
Section B · Revenue Process B1

Pipeline Design: Handoffs & Outbound

Point of view
  • Most funnel leaks aren't a lead-scoring problem. They're a speed and ownership problem at the MQL→SQL seam.
  • Outbound is earned, not decided. It works after ICP is proven and messaging repeats; before that, it burns cash and morale.
Discovery questions
  1. 1A demo request comes in at 4pm Friday. Who touches it, when, and where does Salesforce show it?
  2. 2What are the written acceptance criteria between marketing and sales, and who enforced them last?
  3. 3Can you query untouched leads older than 7 days right now? What's the count?
Learning: You can't fix a handoff you can't see. Lead status + queue + timestamp in Salesforce beats any scoring model at this stage.
Section B · Revenue Process B2

Opportunity Stages & Exit Criteria

Point of view
  • Stages are verifiable buyer actions, not seller optimism. Every stage must answer: “what is TRUE to advance?”
  • Five to seven stages, mapped cleanly to forecast categories, or your forecast call becomes a re-litigation exercise.
Discovery questions
  1. 1Would two of your reps, asked separately, define Stage 3 the same way?
  2. 2Which of your exit criteria are buyer-verified (access to power, agreed success metrics) vs. rep-asserted?
  3. 3How many deals have sat in early stages 60+ days, and what happened to the last one?
Learning: Stage definitions are the backbone of forecast credibility. If stages are opinions, so is your forecast.
Section B · Revenue Process B3

Deal Desk Basics

Point of view
  • You don't need a deal desk team. You need deal desk rules: pricing floors, approval thresholds, standard terms, one home for quotes.
  • Every exception approved over Slack becomes revenue leakage and a comp dispute later.
Discovery questions
  1. 1What discount can a rep give without asking anyone, and where is that written?
  2. 2Does the signed order form always match the Opportunity? When did it last not?
  3. 3What breaks first at 2x deal volume: approvals, legal review, or the billing handoff?
Learning: Approval rules in Salesforce cost a day to build. Unwinding a quarter of founder-approved exceptions costs a lot more.
Section C · Team Design C1

Coverage Maps, Territories & Headcount

Point of view
  • A coverage map is your statement of how the market gets worked: every ICP account has exactly one owner. No gaps, no collisions.
  • Headcount comes from unit math (quota capacity vs. plan, with ramp), not from the size of the raise.
Discovery questions
  1. 1Pick 10 target accounts at random. Can Salesforce name each owner, and would the reps agree?
  2. 2Does booked plan exceed quota × reps × realistic attainment × ramp? By how much?
  3. 3What signal triggers the next sales hire?
Learning: Coverage gaps don't complain. They just don't buy. Account ownership in Salesforce is the map; everything else is folklore.
Section C · Team Design C2

Comp Planning

Point of view
  • Comp is strategy translated into behavior: the plan pays for what you actually want, or you get what it actually pays for.
  • Simple beats clever: one or two measures, clear accelerators, computable in a rep's head.
Discovery questions
  1. 1Can each rep tell you what they'd earn on the deal they're working today, without a spreadsheet?
  2. 2What behavior is the current plan accidentally paying for: sandbagging, discounting, quarter-end cramming?
  3. 3Who models plan cost at 80 / 100 / 120% attainment before it ships?
Learning: If a rep can't compute their check in their head, the plan is managing you, not the other way around.
Section C · Team Design C3

Rep Ramp & Enablement

Point of view
  • Ramp is a measurable asset: milestones at 30/60/90 (first demo, first sourced opp, first close) are your earliest read on both the hire and the system.
  • Enablement at this stage is a living pitch, a stage-by-stage playbook and call review. Not an LMS.
Discovery questions
  1. 1What's your expected ramp to full productivity, and what data produced that number?
  2. 2What must a new rep prove at 30 / 60 / 90 days, and what happened to the last rep who didn't?
  3. 3Who listens to calls, how often, and can you name a change it caused?
Learning: Every month of ramp you shave is a month of quota you don't have to hire for. Ramp time is a comp plan you pay in silence.
Section D · The Operating System D1

Operating Cadences

Point of view
  • Pipeline council, forecast call, QBR: fixed agendas, run from live Salesforce views, not slide-ware.
  • If a meeting can't run from CRM data, it's a status-collection exercise wearing a meeting's clothes.
Discovery questions
  1. 1Which of your recurring revenue meetings run directly out of Salesforce today?
  2. 2How much of a manager's week goes to assembling data vs. inspecting it?
  3. 3What decision does each recurring meeting exist to make?
Learning: The cadence is where RevOps becomes real. Dashboards nobody meets about are decoration.
Section D · The Operating System D2

Forecasting

Point of view
  • Forecasting is trust infrastructure: shared category definitions, a weekly rollup ritual, and someone accountable for the call.
  • Accuracy comes from stage hygiene plus rep-level ownership, not from a fancier model.
Discovery questions
  1. 1Forecast vs. actual for the last four quarters: what were the gaps, and who tracks them?
  2. 2What does “commit” mean here, and would your reps and managers give the same answer?
  3. 3Is the forecast captured in Salesforce (categories, snapshots) or rebuilt in Sheets every Monday?
Learning: A forecast rebuilt in a spreadsheet every week is an admission that nobody trusts the CRM.
Section D · The Operating System D3

CRM Hygiene

Point of view
  • Hygiene equals trust: if leadership can't pull the number without asking a human, the CRM is failing.
  • Every required field must feed a decision. Fewer, better fields, policed by validation rules and stale-deal indicators.
Discovery questions
  1. 1What percentage of open opportunities have a past-due close date or no next step?
  2. 2Which fields are required at each stage, and can you defend why each one exists?
  3. 3How many people have admin rights, and how many hands have shaped the org since inception?
Learning: “Clean Your Room” dashboards beat data-entry lectures. Make hygiene visible and reps police themselves.
Section D · The Operating System D4

Revenue Intelligence & Dashboards

Point of view
  • Every dashboard answers a standing question for one audience. Rep: what do I do today? Manager: where do I coach? Exec: are we on plan?
  • The step-change is marrying product and customer data to the Account (usage, NRR, last sale date) so Salesforce shows revenue truth, not just sales activity.
Discovery questions
  1. 1The five numbers your exec team looks at weekly: do they all come from one source?
  2. 2Which dashboard does each role actually open daily? Name them.
  3. 3What question do you wish you could answer today that you can't?
Learning: Curate, don't boil the ocean. One owned dashboard per audience beats ten orphans.
Section D · The Operating System D5

Cross-Functional Handoffs

Point of view
  • Revenue is a relay: marketing → SDR → AE → CS → finance. Every baton pass needs an owner, entry criteria and a Salesforce artifact.
  • Most “sales problems” at this stage are handoff problems wearing a sales costume.
Discovery questions
  1. 1Map your closed-won handoff: what does CS receive, in which system, and how often is it incomplete?
  2. 2Where do deals stall between functions, and who owns the seam, not just the silo?
  3. 3Do marketing, sales and CS share one vocabulary (ICP, stages, churn reasons) or three private ones?
Learning: Handoffs fail in the white space between org charts. Instrument the seams, not just the stations.
