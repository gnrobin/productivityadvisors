About Us

Good systems are co-authored. The rest just report well.

We help B2B revenue teams build Salesforce orgs that match how they actually sell. We start by listening to your people, then work with them until everyone agrees on what we're building and in what order. The build comes last.

Why we do this

  • We've been on both sides

    Our founder has spent two decades inside revenue teams. Sometimes building the system. Sometimes stuck working around one nobody had asked him about. That second feeling is what we're trying to spare your team.

  • Alignment comes before Setup

    The best systems we've built started with interviews, a few honest arguments, and a written agreement about how the team really runs. Skip that, and you get an org that looks great in a board deck and that nobody actually uses.

  • We take work where judgment matters

    We take engagements where our judgment changes the outcome, and where the outcome is worth reaching. It's why we'll tell you plainly if we're not the right fit.

What we bring

We've run the function, not just advised on it

Our founder owned the GTM stack at Udemy from $5M to $150M+ ARR, growing from 10 reps to 350. We know what breaks at $10M, $30M and $100M because we've had to fix it at each stage, usually while the quarter was still open.

We build with AI, and we're clear about where it helps

We've connected Salesforce to Claude for conversational read and write, built prompt libraries so sellers run their own prospect research, and use AI to draft requirements and enablement material. It speeds up creation. Your team still owns what gets kept.

Are We a Fit?

Teams at one of three moments with Salesforce

Implement

You've outgrown spreadsheets, or a starter org that was stood up fast and never designed. The sales motion is real now, and the system should reflect it.

Re-implement

Years of config built for a motion you no longer run. Every change touches three things nobody documented, and your reps have quietly routed around it.

Expand

Sales lives in Salesforce, but renewals, CS, quoting, partners or marketing still run in tools and spreadsheets that don't connect back.

We'll probably work well together if

  • You'll put the right people in the room, including the skeptics
  • Leadership wants to agree on priorities before the build starts
  • You want your team to own the system after we're gone
  • You'd rather hear the honest answer than the convenient one

We're probably not your team if

  • You need someone to work through a ticket queue without asking why
  • The goal is a dashboard for the board, not a system the team uses
  • Decisions are already made and the stakeholders won't be part of it

Most of our work is with growth-stage teams, by choice. Making twelve people's work better is not a smaller thing than shipping to a thousand strangers. It's just less visible.

Approach

Every engagement runs the same three beats

At whatever scale the work requires.

  1. Step

    Collaborate

    A listening tour first: stakeholder interviews, reverse demos, sitting in on pipeline reviews. We learn your motion before we have an opinion about it.

  2. Step

    Align

    Current state written down, problems separated from symptoms, priorities ranked against your team's real capacity. Leadership signs off on the order of operations.

  3. Step

    Build

    We build with your team, not for them. We pair on the hard configuration, document as we go, and coach your people to own and extend it.

“Organizations that want the artifact without doing the alignment are asking for a lie that reports well.”
The gap

Leadership will call Salesforce strategic, then run the Monday sales meeting off a spreadsheet someone rebuilt Sunday night. That gap isn't a training problem or a data problem. It's a system that was configured before anyone agreed on how the team actually runs.

The change mindset

Whether you're putting in a new system or revamping an existing one, the goal is to be purposeful about getting somewhere better. Collaborate, Align, Build gets stakeholders bought in early, so when the system rolls out, the change is directed and owned by the people who have to live with it.

How we know it worked

Pivoting at the eleventh hour

A fast-growing AI company asked for a minimalist build because their business was still evolving. As requirements came together, it became clear they needed lightweight quoting. Late in the project we pivoted to a CPQ-lite: limited, guardrailed functionality that met the need for where they were, without locking them into a system they'd outgrow.

Both sides felt they won

Sales wants speed, finance wants control, and both are right. We hear each side out and design around what each team actually needs: fast paths for reps, real guardrails for finance. On our best projects, both sides walked away feeling they got what they wanted without giving up too much.
